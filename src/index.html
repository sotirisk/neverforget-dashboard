💡 Spaced Repetition & Memory Timings

Card timings are essential because human memory loses information following an exponential decay curve—a concept known as the Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve.

When you learn a new card, your recall probability drops rapidly within the first 24 to 48 hours. Spaced repetition algorithms (such as SuperMemo SM-2 or FSRS) use precise interval timing to schedule reviews at the optimal moment: right when you are about to forget the answer.

  • Maximizes Long-Term Retention: Reviewing a card precisely when memory strength decays forces the brain to perform active retrieval, which reinforces neural pathways and significantly increases memory stability.
  • Prevents Over-Studying: Without interval timing, you waste time reviewing cards you already know well. Timings push mastered cards further into the future (e.g., from 3 days to 10 days, then 30 days).
  • Prevents Under-Studying: Cards you struggle with are scheduled at shorter intervals (e.g., 10 minutes, 1 day) to catch fading recall before the memory disappears completely.
  • Optimizes Study Efficiency: By scheduling reviews according to individual memory decay rates, you achieve up to 90%+ long-term retention while spending the absolute minimum time studying each day.
